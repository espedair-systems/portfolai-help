To show off your image processing system, you need a mix of images that test different parts of your software.
## Standard Test Images
Engineers have used these classic images for decades because they have a good mix of detail, color, and texture.

* Lena / Mandrill (Baboon): Great for testing sharp details, like whiskers and fur.
* Cameraman: Perfect for testing how your system handles high contrast, shadows, and edges.
* Peppers: Excellent for testing bright colors and smooth shading.

## Photos for Specific Effects
Different image processing tools need different types of pictures to prove they work well. [1] 

* For Blur and Sharpening: Use photos with tiny details, like a page of text, a brick wall, or individual blades of grass.
* For Edge Detection: Use shapes with clean lines, like buildings, geometric patterns, or a dark silhouette against a bright sky.
* For Color Changes: Use colorful scenes, like a rainbow, a fruit basket, or a sunset.
* For Noise Reduction: Use grainy photos taken in low light, or dark night-sky pictures.
* For Resizing and Cropping: Use images with perfect circles or diagonal lines to see if the shapes get distorted or jagged. [2, 3, 4, 5] 

## Diverse Lighting and Subjects
A good system should work on all kinds of real-world photos, not just perfect ones.

* Bright and Dark: Use photos that are accidentally overexposed (too bright) or underexposed (too dark) to test contrast fixing.
* Human Faces: Include portraits with different skin tones to make sure color adjustments look natural.
* Complex Backgrounds: Use busy crowd shots or dense forests to see how your system handles visual clutter. [6, 7, 8]
